POSIX-first ZMODEM

Small tools.
Solid transfers.

zmtx and zmrx are compact command-line programs for sending and receiving files with the ZMODEM protocol. POSIX is the only first-class platform; a defined platform boundary also makes focused ports to smaller systems possible.

What it is

A focused ZMODEM implementation

01

Portable

A strict C99 core with a documented platform boundary. POSIX remains the sole first-class target.

02

Compatible

Binary transfers, CRC16 and CRC32, crash recovery, streaming, Omen ESC8, Pack-7 and negotiated MobyTurbo.

03

Fast

Buffered escaping, batched CRC32, sliding transmit windows and optional 4 KiB or 8 KiB ZedZap blocks.

The source is available under the 2-clause BSD license. Historical third-party notices remain intact.

Where it runs

POSIX first.
Portable by design.

First-class platform

POSIX

Linux, macOS and FreeBSD receive the full performance path, native files and clocks, terminal handling, automated tests, and release-level support.

Bundled secondary port

8-bit CP/M 2.2

Z88DK builds reduced-memory zmtx.com and zmrx.com programs with a replaceable modem driver and generic blocking RDR:/PUN: support. Omen ZMODEM-90 ESC8 and Pack-7 are enabled by default for seven-bit BIOS devices.

Bundled secondary port

16-bit DOS

Open Watcom builds 8086-compatible real-mode programs with full transfer buffers and selectable FOSSIL, interrupt-driven 16550, or BIOS serial I/O. DOSBox and X00 exercise every transport.

Bundled secondary port

32-bit Windows 95

Open Watcom builds 386-compatible Win32 programs for borrowed COM handles or connected Winsock sockets, with optional ZMODEM escaping of Telnet IAC bytes.

Additional systems can implement the documented platform contract without changing the protocol core. These ports are intentionally second-class: they adapt the POSIX-shaped interface to their local compiler, files, clock, and transport facilities.

Where it came from

More than thirty years of history

  1. July 1994

    The original release

    Jacques Mattheij of Mattheij Computer Service publishes zmtx/zmrx as a fresh, compact ZMODEM implementation and programmer-oriented protocol reference.

  2. 2003 onward

    A life inside Synchronet

    Synchronet develops a descendant of this code over many years. Its long history later provides a useful record of protocol fixes that can be brought back to the standalone package.

  3. 19 November 2015

    Copyright transferred

    Jacques Mattheij transfers to Stephen Hurd his copyright and all other rights he held in the released zmodem package and its associated files, naming Fernando Toledo and Rob Swindell as witnesses. The transfer is documented in Synchronet's source history.

  4. August 2026

    The standalone project returns

    The original sources are preserved in Git, relicensed under BSD-2-Clause, modernized for current systems, and reunited with applicable fixes from their Synchronet descendant. Version 2.00 adds ZedZap, non-streaming transfers, sliding windows, performance improvements and extensive protocol tests.

  5. 25 August 2026

    The sexyz benchmark battle

    Receive and transmit paths learn to process ordinary unescaped bytes and CRCs in bulk as zmtx/zmrx goes head-to-head with Synchronet's sexyz. The contest ends in a draw when the test setup reaches its throughput ceiling before either implementation does.
